# SingularityFlow — What It Does
*A plain-language capability summary for leadership. Fuller detail on any
line is available on request; items marked "coming" are designed and
scheduled, not yet built.*

## Keeping control while AI does more of the work
- AI can help with every step — but it cannot approve its own work, skip a step, or declare anything finished. Only verified facts move work forward.
- Every approval names the exact version approved. If the content changes afterward, the approval automatically expires — no one can quietly swap what was signed.
- Risky changes (like payment code) automatically get stricter review; small fixes get a fast lane. The ceremony always matches the risk, without anyone negotiating it.
- Everything still works if the AI is unavailable — proven by automated tests, not promised.

## Always knowing where work stands
- Status is always live. No one writes status updates or schedules status meetings — you open a link that is always right.
- Developers can stop mid-task, switch machines, or go offline, and pick up exactly where they left off. Nothing is ever lost to a dead laptop.
- Managers, reviewers and auditors see the same truth from the same records — there is no "let me check with the team."

## Requirements that actually get enforced
- Acceptance criteria stop being wishes in a ticket: the system checks that every criterion is addressed before work can be submitted.
- When requirements change mid-project (they always do), the system works out exactly which work is affected, reopens only that, and tells everyone what moved and why. Nothing is silently rewritten. *(coming)*
- A developer who discovers a requirement is wrong can formally send it back with their reasoning — recorded, not lost in chat. *(coming)*

## For business people, not just engineers
- Product owners get their own simple view: what needs my decision, my projects, my past decisions — nothing else. *(coming)*
- Rejecting work with a note isn't a comment in a thread — the note becomes the instructions for the next attempt.
- Approvals arrive as notifications with a link; deciding takes one look and one confirmation.

## Answering "is the AI actually helping?"
- Every finished piece of work produces a receipt: how long it took, how much rework, how much AI was involved, what it cost.
- Reports distinguish "faster" from "faster but sloppier" — quality guardrails are built into every comparison.
- No individual surveillance: the system measures work and process, never ranks people.

## Audit and compliance, without the project
- The complete evidence trail — who approved what, when, under which rules — assembles itself as a side effect of working.
- It can be verified offline, from a plain copy of the repository, with no vendor system involved. Three years from now, the proof still works.
- Whether a number, a document, or code was AI-drafted, human-written, or human-reviewed is recorded honestly, every time.

## Fits how we already work
- Runs entirely on tools we already have: Git, VS Code, Copilot, Jira, Teams. No new servers, no new vendor, no data leaving the firm.
- Developers keep their preferred tools and habits; the governance happens at the boundaries, not inside their editor.
- Works with any AI provider — or several at once, with cheaper models automatically used for simple tasks. *(coming)*

## The experience (new users)
- One conversational entry point: ask "what should I do next?" and it tells you — no commands to memorize.
- A refusal is never a dead end: the system lists exactly what's missing and offers the fix.
- New: an "explore first" mode — developers can prototype freely, then one command turns the experiment into properly specified, reviewed, shippable work. *(coming)*

## The bottom line
Prompts generate work. SingularityFlow proves what work happened, under
which rules, and who said yes — and it measures whether the AI is worth
what we're paying for it.
