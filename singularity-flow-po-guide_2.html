01 · Your seat at the table

Singularity Flow for Product Owners

You own intent, priorities, and the final word on what ships. This guide is your side of the system, end to end: express what you want, watch it move without chasing anyone, review the exact thing that was built, and sign it with a signature that means something — while the platform mints the evidence.

What you do here

Bring epics and stories in · answer clarifying questions once · review artifacts against your acceptance criteria · approve or reject on the record · request changes mid-flight without derailing work · read delivery evidence instead of status theater.

What you never do here

Ask “where are we?” in a meeting · write or read status updates · wonder whether what you approved is what shipped · take an AI’s word for anything — an agent structurally cannot approve, and a model’s claim never changes state.

How technical do you need to be? Barely. Your daily home is the Singularity Flow sidebar in VS Code (Inbox · Stories · Lifecycle views), plus Teams links and shareable HTML reports. The terminal commands shown here are the reliable fallback that works from any laptop with a clone — three lines of setup, below.
02 · Three lines, once

Setup

git clone https://git.corp/payments.git && cd payments
sflow doctor          # confirms the machine; fix what it names
sflow inbox           # everything awaiting approval here — you're operational

That is the entire onboarding. No workspace, no capability setup, no Copilot required — the inbox is computed from the repository itself, so a brand-new laptop sees the same list as anyone else’s. The one prerequisite someone must have done for you: your name in the repository’s approvalAuthorities (typically product-reviewers) — that mapping, not any tool, is what gives your approval force.

Reviewing across many repositories? sflow workspace aggregates a capability’s repos into one inbox and one panel — a convenience, never a prerequisite.

03 · Getting work in — mostly in VS Code

Plan epics and shape user stories

Planning lives where you already work: the Singularity Flow sidebar in VS Code. Open the Command Palette (Ctrl/Cmd-Shift-P) and every entry below is a “Singularity Flow:” command.

Connect your sources, once

Connect Jira Securely (credentials go to the OS keychain, never files) and Configure Teams Notifications. Skip Jira entirely if your team plans locally — IDs are minted for you.

Open the epic

Start Work → choose epic intake (Jira epic or local brief). The planning wizard runs the governed stages — intake → requirements → story breakdown — and the assistant asks its open questions before drafting anything. Your brief, decks, and notes attach via Attach Evidence & Designs, each hash-pinned as provenance the drafts must cite.

Shape the story cut — the review board is yours

The drafted breakdown lands in the Stories view as a review package: edit titles and acceptance criteria inline, split a story that’s doing two jobs, adopt an existing ticket into the epic, reorder priorities. The UI is the business review boundary — nothing reaches a developer until you’ve had this pass, and every change reopens the exact planning package, so the record always reflects your final cut, not the model’s first draft.

Release the stories

Approve the breakdown and the stories arrive on developer desks ready to start — each carrying lineage back to your epic, your attached materials, and your acceptance criteria as addressable clauses.

Acceptance criteria are first-class. Write them sharply in the story: they become clauses the implementation must claim, coverage becomes arithmetic the kernel checks, and your later review shows exactly which criterion each change serves. Vague ACs are the one thing this system cannot fix for you.

Terminal equivalents, when you want them: sflow epic start ID --jira · sflow documents upload FILES · sflow epic stories update / split / adopt · sflow epic review.

05 · The craft

Review the exact thing

When a phase awaits you, open it from Approvals (Inbox): Open Artifact shows the exact bytes, Open Reconciliation shows reality-vs-plan, and Specification Traceability walks requirement → change → test → approval. Read the packet in this order:

The artifact, and its hash

You are reviewing exact bytes, identified by SHA-256. What you approve is what ships into evidence — if anyone edits it afterward, your approval goes stale automatically.

Your criteria, claim by claim

Clause coverage shows which acceptance criteria the work claims to satisfy and what remains unclaimed — arithmetic, not narrative. Deviations and escalations arrive as recorded facts with reasons, not surprises.

Who — and what — authored it

Authorship is recorded honestly: human-written, governed-agent generation, or external tool; kernel-invoked AI is observed fact, anything beyond is unknown until attested. You always know whether you’re reading a person or a model, and which questions it asked before drafting.

For mobile work: the pixels

Device-profile coverage (which screens were verified, as a checklist the kernel computed) and pixel-diff evidence against the pinned design version — the screenshot you approve is hash-bound into the record.

06 · The signature

Approve, reject, or request change

Approve — deliberately

Palette → Singularity Flow: Approve…      or: sflow approve PAY-1234

Either surface runs the same ceremony: the artifact, hash, and identity warnings, then requires typing the exact confirmation. Nothing auto-fills it — not the model, not the tool. Your identity, authority group, and the artifact hash bind into one record an auditor can verify offline years later.

Reject — usefully

sflow reject PAY-1234

Your reasons are required — and become pinned context for the next generation: the regeneration literally reads your note. Write the rejection you’d want to receive; specific beats stern. Nothing is lost — the developer’s work, checkpoints, and history carry forward.

Change requests, mid-flight

Scope moved? Request Changes to Completed Story in the palette (or record a change request against the phase) — optionally naming the exact clauses requiring revision. The affected phase reopens with your comment on the record; only work claiming those clauses is disturbed. Changing your mind is a governed act here, not an ambush.

Two things that protect your signature: a waiver (low-risk quick fixes completing under deterministic policy) is always recorded as a waiver — never dressed up as your approval; and self-approval rules mean the person who generated work can be barred from approving it. Your name only ever appears on decisions you actually made.
07 · Evidence, not vibes

Read the delivery evidence

Every completed story mints an Impact Receipt — velocity, quality, AI exposure, cost, completeness — bound to the completion commit. Study comparisons answer the question executives ask you:

sflow impact compare checkout-study

Trust the label, it’s load-bearing

Validated delivery gain means faster and every quality guardrail held. Observed acceleration means faster, full stop — 28% quicker with 24% more rework wears the second label, never the first. The system will not let throughput bury quality.

Check the fine print by design

Every number travels with sample sizes, method, evidence grade (A–D), completeness, and confidence interval — a naked percentage is structurally impossible. Missing data says unavailable; nothing is estimated.

What you’ll never see

Individual rankings. Cohorts below the privacy floor refuse to render, by the engine, regardless of how anyone slices. You get delivery truth; nobody gets a surveillance tool.

08 · Edge of the map

When something needs you unusually

SituationWhat it means · what to do
An escalation lands in your inboxWork outgrew its rail (touched something protected, grew in scope). The stronger path’s approvals now apply — review with the recorded reason; the developer’s work is preserved.
A story was cancelledAn honest ending: artifacts, approvals, and branch remain as reconstructable history. Cancelled never masquerades as complete.
Completed work must changesflow reopen brings it back under governance — no editing history in the dark. Its receipt regenerates on re-completion.
An approval you gave went staleThe artifact changed after you signed. Re-review the new hash; your old signature stays in history, attached to the bytes it actually covered.
Any link is dead / tooling is downA plain terminal always works: clone → sflow inbox → sflow approve. Approvals never require the AI, the panel, or anyone’s uptime.
